The main menu has a new row, between Store and voice over, that says "Vibration, currently on" or "off", and Enter on it switches the controller's vibration. The game remembers it, in settings.json as VIBRATION.
A game controller now vibrates when a zombie hits you, each zombie in its own way: a light punch from the first, scratching pulses from the second and fourth, a heavy mauling from the third, fifth and seventh, a pounce from the sixth, a firm grip when you fail to shake off the animal zombie, a hard smash from the ninth, and a chainsaw that revs up, saws and revs down from the tenth. Shooting the girl who heals you gives a long rumble, and dying gives two hard seconds. Pausing or leaving a game stops it.
A sound and a short vibration tell you when a game controller is found, and another sound when it is disconnected, which also pauses the game. Nothing plays when no controller is found.
A game controller can now play the game. The left stick aims like a swipe: up is 12 o'clock, left 9, right 3, the diagonals 10:30 and 1:30, and down reloads. One lean is one shot, and the MG80 keeps firing while the stick is held. The D-pad works like the arrow keys: left, up and right fire at 9, 12 and 3, left and up together at 10:30, right and up together at 1:30, and down reloads. X reloads, the bumpers change weapon, A shakes off a zombie, and B or Start pauses and resumes.
A game controller now works on the menus and the shop: the D-pad or left stick moves, A selects and B goes back. It uses the same standard layout on Windows, Linux and macOS.
The MG80 now keeps firing while you hold its lane key, like a machine gun. Every other weapon still fires once per press.
The MG80 can now fire again after 0.085 seconds instead of 0.4, and its shots overlap instead of cutting each other off.
The game is now called Sixth Sense Reborn: the window and the main menu say so, the game is SixthSenseReborn.exe, and its download is SixthSenseReborn-Win-<version>.zip.
Sixth Sense Reborn keeps its own save, in a SixthSenseReborn folder, so it never changes your Sixth Sense save. The first time it starts, it copies your Sixth Sense progress, settings and keys.
Added macOS support with a copyable app and VoiceOver or native speech; targets macOS 11 or newer.
